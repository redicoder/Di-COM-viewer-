## Data flow

1. A scan (DICOM zip/folder or NRRD) goes through `dicom_converter_app.convert()` (window, CLI or the
   viewer's Open scan): load -> crop to patient -> resample (voxel budget per quality level) -> remove
   table -> `out/<name>/volume.raw + volume.json` -> optional TotalSegmentator `labels.raw + labels.json`
   -> optional lungs-only copy -> `out/latest.txt` lists the result folders.
2. The viewer's `VolumeLoader` reads `latest.txt` (found relative to the project or the build), builds a
   UnityVolumeRendering `VolumeDataset` with identity rotation (texture x = patient left, y = superior,
   z = posterior), adds organ labels as a segmentation, applies the transfer-function preset.

## Dissection bodies (`tools/build_body.py`)

1. `prepare_visible_human.py` turns the downloaded CT parts into one NRRD: slices ordered by the Visible
   Human slice number (`SliceLocation`), every slice resampled by its own pixel size and position onto one
   0.94 mm grid (the head was reconstructed with a smaller field of view), parts shifted to line up at the joins.
2. `build_body.py` finds the body, runs TotalSegmentator `total` **once** on a 1.5 mm whole-body grid
   (cached in `out/<body>/_cache/seg`), then builds each export: whole body at 2 mm and head, thorax,
   abdomen, pelvis at about 1 mm (≤ 40 M voxels). Region heights come from landmarks (C4, lungs and
   clavicles, L5 to liver/spleen, hip bones); width and depth from the body inside them.
3. Per export: CT resampled onto the export grid (all grids are boxes in mm in the scan's frame, so they line
   up); the patient = connected pieces overlapping the whole-body mask; TotalSegmentator labels cleaned of
   stray pieces (< 0.5 % of a structure), mapped by position and smoothed; tissue classes from HU (`app/tissue.py`) fill the rest; ids from
   `app/label_table.py`, which never renumbers (`tests/label_ids.json` is the snapshot).
4. `labels.json` = `layers` (peel order, names, colour) + `labels` (id, key, layer, `name` in English for the
   current viewer, `names` {la, en, it}, colour, opacity, info_key). `provenance.json` = dataset, licence,
   credit, source hash, grid, thresholds, tool versions, git commit.

## Photographed body (`tools/prepare_photo_volume.py`)

The Visible Human male was frozen, cut into 1 mm slices and every slice photographed (2048 × 1216 RGB, 0.33 mm
pixels, body in blue gelatin). Each photo's `SliceLocation` is the same slice number as his frozen CT.

**The photographs are lined up first** (`tools/register_photos.py`, 2026-11-17). They come in runs: within a run every
picture lies on the one before it; from one run to the next the whole picture is moved sideways by whole pixels. In the
male that happens six times between the hips and the head (photographs 1729, 1416, 1390, 1360, 1252, 1230: jumps of -5,
-13, +12, -13, +11 and -9 pixels, 1.7 to 4.3 mm). Stacked as they come, the body has a step right round it at each. The
tool measures every photograph against the one before it (phase correlation of the tissue's lightness, the grey bar and
the number card left out), takes a run to begin where one photograph lies 3 pixels or more off what its neighbours on
both sides say AND the picture's left and right halves, measured apart, both show it (where the two legs or the two
sides of the skull's top move differently, the whole picture's correlation hops between them: five such lone "jumps"
of a millimetre were no jump of the picture and are left alone), and writes `registration.json` into the photographs'
folder: for every slice number the whole pixels its picture is to be moved by to lie as the feet's run does.
`prepare_photo_volume.read_photo` moves every picture by it, and EVERYTHING that reads photographs goes through that one
function (the two exports, the SIO labels' import, the fine skin, the fine muscles), so all of it is built on the stack
as lined up; `prepare_photo_volume.py` warns when a folder has no registration and records `registered` in `photo.json`.
Checked against two sources that were made straight by others: the SIO's own crop of the same photographs (aligned by
its makers to the CT) stepped on our stack at the same four photographs of the trunk by -6, -18, -6, -18 pixels (to its
3 pixel voxel; ours: -5, -18, -6, -19) and is now one constant corner over all its 774 slices; and the frozen CT's place
on the photographs along the trunk stepped at three of them and no longer does. Not found by this: a jump of about a
millimetre, and a block of the specimen laid a little aside from the one under it (the photographs at the joints are
blank or not whole, and cannot be measured across).

An export made before the photographs were lined up, all of whose photographs lie in one run, need not be made again:
`register_photos.py <photos> --export <folder>` moves its record of where it was cropped from by that run's move and
leaves its voxels. The head export is kept that way (+17 pixels), on purpose: it dates from 2026-10-22 and the tool has
changed since (it equalises every photograph's exposure now); made afresh, the eyes are measured elsewhere and the
face's frame with them (docs/SPEC_NOTES.md). Everything measured on the head is tuned on the export as it is.

The export tool
flips the rows to the CT's layout (front to back), crops to the body, averages down (factor 2 = 0.66 mm), keeps
tissue (redder than blue) as the body's 3D pieces (every piece at least 2 % of the largest, connectivity judged across the
joints between the specimen's blocks; a piece that does not change from photo to photo is the label card beside him,
not tissue) with holes filled, repairs the sliver and blank photos at the block joints from their neighbours (a sliver lacks a rim of the body that both its neighbours have), matches each block's exposure to the next across its joint on the skin (12 cm either side, then an exact ramp at the joint), leaves gelatin-filled air spaces empty, gives the
outermost voxel of tissue the colour beneath it (it was skin averaged with the gelatin) and bleeds tissue colour into
the gelatin, equalises each photo's exposure to its neighbours' (on all its tissue, over nine photos), makes the outer surface a 1.5 mm smoothing of the
filled outline (the inside stays sharp), and writes `photo.raw` (RGBA, x = patient left, rows front to back, slices bottom
to top) + `photo.json`; `--web` writes JPEG colour + PNG alpha atlases for the browser sample. The renderer finds
the first tissue sample along each ray: on a cut face it shows the photo itself; on the skin it shades the
surface and reads the colour 0.7 mm below it, blended over neighbouring slices. `--every 2 --factor 4` exports the
whole body at 1.3 x 2 mm (`out/photo_body`, 436 x 282 x 939 voxels, 460 MB RGBA; `LargeVolumeTests` checks a volume
that size reaches the GPU whole); the head at 0.66 mm is `out/photo_head`. Three tools work on an export afterwards: `extract_skin_mesh.py` (the skin as a mesh), `transfer_eyes.py` (the head's eyes and its box into the whole body) `import_sio_labels.py` (the SIO organ labels, registered slice by slice; unlabelled tissue inside the labelled torso takes the nearest label, so a layer taken away leaves no specks) and `classify_tissue.py` (tissue classes for the rest of him and for the head export: a table indexed by smoothed colour, depth under the skin and local unevenness, filled from the labelled torso's twenty million voxels and scored on held-out slices; the classes go in as five more objects, "skin (by colour)" to "organ (by colour)", so the layers work on the head and legs too) and `import_ct_bone.py` (bone from his frozen CT, over 250 HU with marrow filled, each slice placed on its photograph by outline as the SIO was: "bone (from CT)" wherever the SIO has no name, because at 1.3 mm the photographs show bone as marrow and the colour table misses most of it).

## Anatomy Lab (`unity/Assets/DicomViewer/Scripts/AnatomyLab`)

The app opens on a start screen (`AppMode`): **Scan viewer** (everything below) or **Anatomy lab**. Each mode is its
own object and only the chosen one is active; the lab's objects are built in code (`AnatomyLab.CreateRig`), so the
scene is unchanged. `-mode lab` / `-mode viewer` on the command line skips the menu.

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| `PhotoBody` | loads `backend/out/<body>/body.raw + body.json` (`prepare_photo_volume.py --unity`; `photo_head` or `photo_body`, the toolbar's **Body** button swaps them) into an sRGB RGBA 3D texture, an eyelid map and an empty-space map (one cell per 8^3 voxels); CPU picking that respects cuts. Its transform is the body's (metres, x left, y up, z back); the child cube is scaled to the body, so cube-local = VolumeSpace's convention |
| `Shaders/AnatomyLab/PhotoBody.shader` + `LabSkin.hlsl` (+ `LabBody.hlsl`) | first-hit ray march with empty-space skipping; cut walls show the photo itself, skin takes its colour from 0.7 mm under the surface (the epidermis, blended over neighbouring slices) and its normal over 1.2 mm (cut walls keep the fine one); the eyes open geometrically (an almond fissure whose lids slide into the orbit, continuous with the skin round it, no mask edge), the real eyeballs can be swapped for modelled ones; blood stains, soft occlusion in folds; writes SV_Depth so meshes and drops mix in. `LabSkin.hlsl` is the lighting shared with lifted skin: key light with subsurface wrap and a blood-warm terminator, fill and rim set against the camera, hemisphere ambient, GGX specular over a procedural micro-relief, wet where cut or bloody, a soft shoulder on highlights, and a mild colour grade (`LabSkinGrade`) that lifts the blue bath's cast off the frozen skin |

| `SkinMesh` + `LabSkinMesh.shader` + `LabBody.hlsl` | the skin as a mesh over the volume (`skin.mesh`, from `tools/extract_skin_mesh.py`: marching cubes on the skin-depth map, Taubin smoothing, decimation to 200-400k triangles): lit and shadowed like a game character. By default (`_SkinMode` 1, the toolbar's **Skin: clean**) it is one even skin tone over his real shape with the slow unevenness living skin has, a flush where the skin is thin, and briefs between the hip bones and the thighs (`AnatomyLab.Briefs`, from the labels); **Skin: photos** reads the colour from the photographs under the surface instead, graded to a living tone. It cuts itself away where the mask, a lifted sheet or the eyes' opening say so, and the volume beneath (told `_SkinByMesh`) draws only what is opened, lifted or deeper, so cutting still reveals the real tissue. `LabBody.hlsl` holds what the two shaders share. A mesh skin does not stretch (the Pull tool's stretch is off with it) |
| `LabRegion` | regions at full detail: where the whole body's export names a finer export inside it (`regions` in body.json, written by `tools/transfer_eyes.py`: the head at 0.66 mm), that export is loaded as a body of its own with its skin mesh, at its place inside the whole body; the whole body's volume and mesh leave the box to it (`_RegionOn/_RegionMin/_RegionMax`), and the tools look away from it (to dissect the face, show the head on its own) |

| `ModelLab` + `ModelOrbit` + `Editor/ModelSnapshot.cs` | the **modelled body**: GLB models in `Assets/Models/` (the body, then the skeleton, the muscles and each organ, generated from reference pictures by an image-to-3D tool; imported by `com.unity.cloud.gltfast`) in a plain world frame (up +y, the table top y = 0, he lies along x with his head at -x, face up). `ModelLab.Lay` scales a model to real height and lays it on its back; `ModelLab.Room` builds the steel table and the floor as lit meshes (URP Lit) that receive his shadow; the generator's material is corrected (it exports skin as full metal). `ModelOrbit` walks the camera round the table (drag, pinch or wheel, two fingers or right-drag to slide; the room stays put). `ModelBody` holds the layers (skin `body.glb`, `muscles.glb`, `skeleton.glb`, outermost first) laid at the same place; the generated models do not nest exactly, so only the outermost layer that is on is drawn, and buttons at the top of the screen pick the layer to see. `ModelPolish` bakes each layer when the scene starts (under a second each): normals smoothed across the texture seams and, per vertex, occlusion, the tissue behind the surface and curvature, from the layer's own surface in a voxel grid (stored as `SkinBake` stores them). `LabModel.shader` lights the layers with the lab's tissue lighting (`LabSkin.hlsl`, `LabShadeTissue`: scattering skin with the pre-integrated lookup, wet muscle, matte pitted bone), reads the texture's brightness as relief (no normal map), darkens and warms the crevices, and tones down the generator's bright textures. The body the scene shows is the **anatomy** (`Assets/Models/anatomy.glb`, from `backend/tools/build_model_meshes.py`): every labelled structure of the photographed body's export (SIO structures, tissue classes, CT bone) as its own smooth mesh in metres (blurred voxels, marching cubes, decimation; about 190 structures, 1.4 M triangles), under four layer nodes skin (with briefs) / muscles / skeleton / organs, with an atlas colour per structure and the bake (occlusion from a blurred occupancy of the layer, thickness from the body's distance field) in the vertex colours; the layers come from one body, so they fit inside one another and are drawn together (`ModelBody.nested`). The builder is the package `backend/tools/anatomy_model/` (`build_model_meshes.py` is its command; each layer is cached in `<export>/model/` so one layer can be rebuilt alone, and `anatomy_model.preview` renders any of it to a picture without Unity). **Skin** (`fine_skin`, `skin`): the body's outline is read again from every photograph at 0.66 x 1 mm (twice the export's detail), guided by the export's cleaned mask, as a coverage and not a yes/no mask (the ring of pixels round the tissue says how much of it is skin); the surface is contoured slab by slab, relaxed within half a pixel (`common.relax`: the terraces a pixel grid leaves become the slope they came from; at the joints between the specimen's blocks a couple of millimetres), and decimated to 600k triangles with the face, hands and feet magnified first so they keep about twice the detail. What lies under the skin is clipped to the fine skin's inside. The outline is found at the photographs' own 0.33 mm pixels and kept as each 0.66 mm voxel's share of body, so the surface sits within a fraction of a pixel. The decimation is by region with the regions' borders kept (`common.decimate_regions`, pyfqmr): about 0.8 mm edges on the face and ears, 1.4 mm on the hands, 1.8 mm on the feet and the rest of the head, about 3.3 mm on the body, 870k triangles. On the face the relaxation is held to 0.3 mm, and its fine lines (where the lids meet, between the lips, the nostrils' rims) are shaded from how far each vertex lies below its neighbours on the finished mesh. **Zones** (`zones`): landmarks from the data (eye centres, the face's profile for nose and lip line, sternum, arm and leg bones) give per-vertex weights for lips, lids, brows, scalp, beard, areolae, chest, belly line, arms, legs, soles; the lid line of each eye is read from the skin's relief; the middle of the face takes its light and dark from his own photographs (the head export's colour a few millimetres under the skin, blurred, as a ratio against his cheeks over the clean tone: the lips as they are, the brown round the eyes, the beard's shadow), the body keeps the clean tone; from them the skin's colour, gloss and hair mask per vertex (in the texture-coordinate channels; there is no UV map). **Eyelids and eyes** (`lids`, `eyes`, `LabLids.hlsl`): the skin is cut along each lid line (the furrow nearest to where lids meet, 7 mm below the globe's middle on this man) from corner to corner; the seam's vertices are moved onto the line and doubled, a strip of wet margin gives the lid's edge its thickness, and every lid vertex carries how much it follows the edge (TEXCOORD_2: positive on the upper lid, negative on the lower; which eye). The shaders of the skin and of the lashes turn a lid vertex about its eye's centre by its weight times the opening, so the edge travels and the skin behind it gathers into the fold. The globes are measured again in the head's photographs (the dark glassy body of each eye: centre and radius) and built as spheres with a cornea, coloured per vertex (a dead man's eye: dull sclera, grey-brown iris, wide pupil, a little clouded); the layers under the skin are cleared in a space round each globe (22 mm about the gaze's axis, from the lids to a little behind the globe's middle), and that space's own walls, a millimetre in and without the side that is the skin, are the conjunctiva (`eyes.lining`): a look in through an opened eye ends on the globe or on the lining and never on the fat behind (a lining shaped on its own, a cup, left gaps wherever the face had no room for it). The skin above the upper lid moves with it up to the brow (21 mm: the lid's edge travels 13 mm, and the skin must give way no faster than the edge comes or the edge runs into it) and stands out as a fold on the way (the fold's height rides on the eye's code in TEXCOORD_2). The lids' values (`_EyeR`, `_EyeL`, `_EyeFwd`, `_EyeUp`, `_LidOpen`) are material properties inside each shader's `UnityPerMaterial` buffer (`LAB_LID_UNIFORMS`): left outside it, the SRP batcher takes them for globals in a batchable shader and ignores the material, which is how the lashes once stayed down while their lid went up. `ModelSnapshot.RunEyes` renders the eyes alone in about a minute (`build/snapshots/eye_*.png`). `anatomy.json` beside the GLB hands Unity what the importer does not (eye centres, the face's axes, the lids' turn); `ModelBody` takes a lid with the pointer on the eye and draws it towards the brow (it stays where it is left), and has an Open eyes button. `--skin-reuse` rebuilds zones, lids and hair on the last surface in a minute. **Hair** (`hair`): about 130,000 strands as ribbons on their centre lines (brows, lashes, stubble of scalp and beard, hair of chest, belly line, arms, legs), scattered by area and region weight, grown along the body's hair streams; `LabHair.shader` gives each its width facing the camera, never under a pixel, with the real coverage in alpha, and drops strands by seed with distance. `LabModel.shader` reads the vertex colour, adds skin's own unevenness (blotches, mottle, pores, follicles where hair grows) and `LabSkin.hlsl` now has two specular lobes and a red edge to shadows on skin. A **fat** layer lies between skin and muscles. **Skeleton** (`bonehub`): the 142 bones BoneHub labels on this same man's CT (0.94 x 0.94 x 1 mm) are meshed one by one on their own grid and moved into the body by a fit of the two bone masks (a shift per axis; the bones then lie within 1 to 3 mm of ours in the trunk, up to 8 mm in the arms, which sit differently in the CT and in the photographs); our own labels keep the cartilages and discs, and bone of ours with nothing of BoneHub's within 12 mm fills its gaps (the elbows), unnamed. ## Dissection mask

`_MaskTex` (R8, same grid as the CT, 1 = tissue) is bound to the forked volume shader (see the package's
`MODIFICATIONS.md`). Every edit is a `MaskOp` in millimetres: capsules (scalpel, dig; a stroke is one op) or a
half-space (knife). The main thread applies each op on the GPU immediately; the CPU copy follows on a background
lane with cloned ops. Undo/redo/reset replay the op list from a full body on both sides; both use the same voxel
ranges (`MaskModel.CapsuleRange`), and `MaskGpuTests` checks they agree.

UI is IMGUI (`OnGUI`) for now; the spec moves it to UI Toolkit (step 6).
