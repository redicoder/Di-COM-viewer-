# Spec notes: agreed deviations and open checks

Review of `DISSECTION_TABLE_SPEC.md` before implementation. Each item is either a decision (applied) or a
check that must be done before the related step is closed.

## Decisions

| Spec | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| §2.1 fork UVR into `Assets/ThirdParty/` | Kept as an **embedded package** in `unity/Packages/com.mlavik1.easyvolumerenderer/`, git-URL dependency removed from the manifest, changes logged in its `MODIFICATIONS.md` | Same effect (code in the repo, editable), but assembly definitions and asset GUIDs stay identical to upstream |
| §2.2 shader snippet (`sampler3D`, `tex3Dlod`) | Written in the URP shader's own style: `Texture3D` + `SamplerState` + `SampleLevel` | The URP DVR shader declares textures that way; mixing styles does not compile cleanly |
| §2.5 GPU picking first | First a **CPU copy of the mask**, updated by the same `MaskOp` list, used by the existing CPU ray-march picker behind `IVolumePicker`; GPU pick pass later if profiling asks for it | Picking must see cuts from day one; the CPU path is simpler and testable, and runs only on tap |
| §2.6 dig coalescing | A dig stroke is stored as **one op** (path of centres + radius), not one op per frame | Keeps the replay list short for undo/redo and lessons |
| §3.1 Visible Human **male** first | The **female** is the primary demo body: her fresh-cadaver CT (IDC study "Normal") is 1 mm from head to toe. The male's 1 mm CT is the *frozen* one: freezing brings fat and muscle within ~25 HU of each other (fat ≈ −50, muscle ≈ −25), so the HU tissue classes (§3.2 step 3) cannot separate them; his fresh CT is 3–5 mm. The male stays buildable (`--dataset vhp-male-frozen`, with his own `--fat` / `--muscle` ranges) | The layers (skin, fat, muscle) need real tissue contrast |
| §1 / §3.1 what the student sees (product owner, 2026-10-20) | The app gets two modes: **Scan viewer** (DICOM/NRRD → 3D, as built) and **Anatomy lab**, a body that looks and behaves like a real cadaver. After a side-by-side sample (game-style mesh vs photographs), the owner chose the **photographed body**: the Visible Human **male**'s colour cryosection photos (0.33 mm pixels, one per 1 mm slice), stacked into an RGBA volume (`tools/prepare_photo_volume.py`). His frozen CT has the same slice numbering and state, so CT labels (names, picking, layers) map onto the photos slice by slice. Touch physics (pinch, flaps, eyelids), blood (labelled as a simulation; a cadaver does not bleed) and organ removal are built on top, step by step | Solid real tissue in real colour wherever it is cut; meshes are hollow and invented inside. This supersedes "female first" (next row) for the Anatomy lab |
| URP renderer (PC_Renderer) | **Screen Space Ambient Occlusion switched off** | It forces a depth-normals prepass that ray-marched volumes (the scan viewer's and the photographed body) are not part of, so `_CameraDepthTexture` missed the body and liquid blood showed through it; the body shader does its own fold shading. With it off URP copies the real depth after opaques |
| §3.1 the body must look real before anything done to it can (product owner, 2026-10-22) | Three things in the export and one in the shader: each photo's exposure is equalised to its neighbours (the rings round the head were slice-to-slice brightness), the body's outer surface is a 1.5 mm smoothing of the filled outline (the inside stays sharp: sinuses, cut neck), the outermost voxel of tissue takes the colour beneath it (it was skin averaged with the blue gelatin, hence green), and the skin is lit as skin (`LabSkin.hlsl`). The frozen cadaver's own colour is kept, with a slight warm correction for the blue bath's cast | A photographed body at 0.66 mm can look like a face only with a smooth surface and real skin lighting; the data decides the colour |
| §3.1 the face on the whole body (product owner, 2026-10-22: "still not that good") | On the whole body the head is drawn from the **head export** (0.66 mm) in place of the 1.3 mm data, as a region (`LabRegion`); the skin's colour is averaged over 2 mm of surface (the frozen photographs are blotchy) with the detail in the relief; the living grade kept mild | The face is what a student looks at; 1.3 mm cannot be a face. Regions are the spec's answer to detail on a whole body |
| §3.1 "a 3D game-like body, like a GTA 5 character" (product owner, 2026-10-22) | **This real man, rendered like a game character**, not an artist's idealised body: his skin extracted as a mesh from the photo volume and shaded with a game-quality skin material, graded to a living tone, open living eyes; the volume stays beneath for everything done to him. Outside first, then the inside (organs from the volume and the CT labels) | A stock character would be nobody, and its inside would not match its outside; students study a real body. The mesh gives shadows, occlusion and frame rate the ray-march cannot |
| §3.1 learn from open projects that made a real-looking body (product owner, 2026-10-22) | Looked at: the **Visible Korean** virtual dissection table (Anatomage: the sectioned photographs as a volume, surface models, labels on both; the same plan as ours), **Z-Anatomy** / BodyParts3D and the Leiden **Open 3D Model** (generic meshes, CC BY-SA: share-alike, and not this man; not used), the open **Unity human skin shader** (separable screen-space scattering; forward-only, Unity 2021), and the **Segmented Internal Organs of the Visible Human Male** (SIO, Höhne et al., UKE Hamburg, **CC BY 4.0**, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.15882018: 202 labelled structures of the torso at 1 mm, made from the same photographs). Adopted: (1) the game skin pipeline's baked lighting, but baked from his own volume (`SkinBake`: occlusion, thickness, curvature) and pre-integrated scattering instead of a screen-space pass (no extra render pass, works in the ray-marched scene); (2) real cast shadows on the skin mesh; (3) the SIO labels registered onto his photographs (`tools/import_sio_labels.py`), so the inside has real names from day one: the lab names what is under the cursor and lifts that structure in a cut. The SIO crop steps sideways between the specimen's blocks (it was aligned to the CT block by block), so every slice is registered and a running median keeps the steps | A real cadaver for students, not a character: the open projects that look real all draw the photographs; their labels are the one thing ours lacked. CC BY data can ship with credit; CC BY-SA meshes would bind the product |
| §3.1 / §3.2 layers over the labelled body (2026-10-22) | With the SIO labels in place, the lab's first way inside is **layers**: skin, fat, muscle, bone, organ (each object's layer from its name), taken away one at a time over the torso with toolbar buttons; the volume and the skin mesh skip the hidden voxels and the tools treat them as gone, so the muscles and then the organs show in place, in his photographs, with their names under the cursor. The head and legs lie outside the SIO and keep their skin until they are labelled (TotalSegmentator on his CT, §3.2) | What the spec's layers (§3.2 step 3) promise, from real labels instead of HU classes: the frozen CT could not separate fat from muscle |
| §3.1 the whole body (product owner, 2026-10-22: "bring the whole body", then "it doesn't feel real") | The whole male at **1.3 x 2 mm** (every 2nd photo, 4 x 4 pixel blocks, 115 M voxels, 460 MB RGBA; 2 mm looked like a figurine) beside the head at 0.66 mm; the lab switches between them. He lies on his back on a steel table in a room (floor, his shadow on the table), down is towards the table however he is turned. The legs were missing at first: the specimen's block joints break 3D connectivity, and the label card photographed beside him runs through every photo, so the body is "the largest piece and every big piece that continues it along the stack, not beside it". The joints between his blocks (blank or sliver photos, a step in exposure) are repaired from the photo above and matched on the skin; a hairline at the hip remains where the two blocks disagree about his outline. No eyes found at this resolution, so the whole body has his real closed eyes only | 0.66 mm for the whole body would be 1.2 GB of texture. Regions at full detail (as the head) are the way to the rest of him |
| §3.1 the whole body's skin colour (2026-10-22: the belly mottled grey-green) | **Tried and reverted.** The skin is a 1-2 mm band in the photographs; at 1.3 mm voxels the outer voxel mixes it with the fat beneath and the rim at the gelatin, and over the belly the frozen surface is frosted grey-green in patches. Two recolourings were tried: the skin band read per slice from its own photograph (streaked the body: neighbouring slices chose different skin) and a 3D evening of the outer shell from "skin-coloured" voxels around it (blotched it: lividity and pooled blood pass any colour rule for skin, and spread). The photographs' colour stays as it is | A colour rule cannot tell his skin from his bruises; the mottling is real data. The way to an even skin is finer voxels at the surface (regions at 0.66 mm, as the head) or a skin texture baked from the photographs onto the mesh, not a recolouring of the volume |
| §3.1 the outer body like a dissection table's (product owner, 2026-10-23, with a photo of one: "the photographic outer body is not a good option") | The skin mesh is drawn by default as **one even skin tone over his real shape** (the table look): a pale tone with the slow unevenness living skin has, a flush where the skin is thin (ears, nose, lips), real shadows and occlusion, and a pair of briefs placed from the labels (hip bones to below the genitals). The photographs stay beneath: every cut, the layers and the names are still his. **Skin: photos** on the toolbar brings the photographed surface back | The frozen photographs' surface (frost, lividity, block seams) cannot be made even by recolouring (row above); his shape is real and that is what the mesh keeps. Where the owner wants the photographs is inside, where they are right |
| §3.1 skin that is cut and pulled (product owner, 2026-10-21: "treat it like what will happen on a real body") | Skin taken in hand is **simulated, not animated**: a two-layer particle sheet with the tissues' real moduli, an anisotropic fat-to-fascia junction that tears when lifted or slid too far, shape matching for the dermis's bending, gravity and contact (`TissueSheet`, Burst job). A single incision only lets its edge be lifted, a U-shaped cut makes a flap that peels to its base and hangs, a closed cut frees a piece that comes away in the hand and falls onto a tray. The earlier analytic "fold about a hinge" flap is gone | Owner's choice; the physics gives every case (undermining, hanging, falling) from one model instead of one special case each |
| Hard, self-contained graphics parts | Written by **Claude Fable** from the prompts in `docs/prompts/` (living eye, liquid blood, tissue pulling), reviewed and integrated here; each plugs in through a fixed interface (`LivingEye.Create`, `BloodDrops`, `WarpUVW`) | Owner's choice; the interfaces keep the parts swappable |
| §3.1 whole body ≈ 61 M voxels | The female's body box at 2 mm is ≈ 31 M voxels; regions at 1 mm, budget 40 M each | Measured on the data |
| §3.2 step 1 NRRD/NIfTI via SimpleITK or nibabel | Our own streaming NRRD reader (`app/nrrd_loader.py`, tested against DICOM); NIfTI not yet | No extra dependency in the main environment |
| §3.2 step 2 per export | TotalSegmentator runs **once per body** on a 1.5 mm whole-body grid (its native resolution); every export maps those labels by position in mm | Exports agree with each other, regions get whole-body context, one GPU run instead of five |
| §3.2 step 3 "other soft tissue: remainder" | Gas inside the body (sinuses, mouth, bowel gas outside labelled bowel) stays 0 = empty; fat and muscle are judged on a 1 mm Gaussian-smoothed copy of the CT; "other soft tissue" belongs to the **muscle** layer | Gas must not render as solid; noise would otherwise speckle fat/muscle; soft tissue must peel away before the organs show |
| §2.3 / §3.3 `labels.json` `name` = {la, en, it} | `name` stays a plain English string (the current viewer reads it with JsonUtility) and the three languages are in `names`; labels also carry `key`. The viewer moves to `names` with the language switch (step 5) | Each step leaves the app working |
| §3.3 `backend/data/anatomy_names.json` | `backend/anatomy/anatomy_names.json` (and later `anatomy_info.json`) | `backend/data/` is git-ignored: it holds scans |
| §4 legacy `Input` removed | The **Input Manager stays enabled** (Active Input Handling = Both) even after the tools move to the Input System | Sony's SR Display plugin reads the legacy Input Manager |
